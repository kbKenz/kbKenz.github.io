Bektur Kenzhebaev

I am a computer science student at the American Institute of Technology, Salymbekov University, a research collaborator with ARCIDS Lab at Wuhan University, and a founding AI engineer at Verc. I work at the boundary between machine learning research and production AI systems, with an interest in how intelligent systems learn, act, coordinate, and improve.

My interests span trustworthy agentic AI, evaluation, alignment, human oversight, and the engineering of reliable systems. I am also interested in how these ideas transfer to robotics, recommender systems, and other sequential decision problems. A recurring thread in my work asks how we can understand behavior over time, locate the source of a failure, and design better interventions.

I am preparing for thesis-based graduate study beginning Fall 2027 and am open to research collaborations across machine learning, AI safety, robotics, recommender systems, and related areas. Please contact me at kenzhebaevbektur@gmail.com.

Research direction

One current research thread examines what happens when an agent’s behavior unfolds through a chain of decisions and interactions. Modern failures are often visible only after that chain has already shaped the outcome.

Can an agent’s trajectory history reveal the first meaningful deviation, and can that evidence support intervention before a misaligned strategy propagates through a multi-agent system?

Measure what emerges

Evaluate repeated interaction and system equilibrium, where individually acceptable behavior can become collectively harmful.

Find the first deviation

Use trajectory representations that separate an initiating error from inherited drift, downstream symptoms, and genuinely joint causes.

Intervene while it matters

Use pre-execution signals to decide when an agent or coordinator should pause, redirect, or request review.

This thread grows out of recent submitted work on pre-execution trajectory control and failure attribution in iterative LLM workflows, alongside my accepted work on equilibrium-level harm in multi-agent systems. It is one part of a broader interest in building AI systems that can be evaluated, understood, and improved.

  • When Call-Level Attribution Fails in Iterative LLM Workflows: Representation, Calibration, and Non-Singleton Repairs

    Submitted manuscript · August 2026

    Submitted manuscript · August 2026

    Working manuscript · 2026

  • Ghost Knowledge in Agent Memory: Auditing Behavioral Persistence After Deletion and Correction

  • Checkpoint Invalidation in Stateful LLM Agents: Behavioral Residue After Memory Updates

  • Certifying Component-Wise Audits Against Harmful Multi-Agent Equilibria

    Unpublished manuscript · 2026

  • Equilibrium-Level Harm in Multi-Agent AI Ecosystems: Why Ethical Design Cannot Stop at the Individual Agent
